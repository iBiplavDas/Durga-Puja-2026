# Reel brief: “एक फल, दो भाई” (Ganesha and Kartikeya’s race)

## 1. The ask

Make a 9:16 vertical Instagram Reel, about 75 seconds, that tells the story of Ganesha and Kartikeya’s race around the world. The story is carried by Hindi voiceover. The visuals show exactly what the voice is saying, shot by shot. On-screen text is minimal: two to four words at a time, plus one big Hindi phrase as the hook. It ends on a gentle life lesson and one small thing the viewer can do today.

## 2. Brand and audience

- **Page:** an Instagram page about the stories of India’s gods, for all of India (handle @pujoparikramaguide; name to be decided).
- **Audience:** 20 to 40, anywhere in India, comfortable with Hindi, scrolling at night, looking for calm, meaning and a little guidance, not preaching.
- **Promise:** tell an old story well, say where it comes from, then turn it gently toward the viewer’s own life.
- **Voice:** a wise friend, not a preacher. Calm, warm, short sentences. “कहते हैं” (it is said) instead of stating legend as fact. Hope, never fear. No “share or bad luck”, no miracle claims, no politics.
- **Look:** real Indian paintings (Kalighat, Pahari, Rajasthani, Ravi Varma-era prints), brought to life with slow camera moves, cut-out figures moving across a scene, moonlight, drifting specks of light, glows. Deep night-indigo (#0E1228), ivory (#F4ECDC) and muted gold (#CCA862) around the paintings. No neon, no saffron gradients, no glossy 3D or “AI glow” gods.
- **Type:** Devanagari in a classical serif (Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit) in gold for the one big phrase; small spaced capitals (Poppins Medium) for labels.
- **Sound:** one narrator over a single soft instrument (bansuri flute), low. No trending audio.

## 3. The story and its sources

**Version told (popular southern telling, also widely known across India):** Narada brings a unique fruit, the fruit of knowledge, to Kailash. Shiva and Parvati say whoever goes around the whole world first and returns will get it. Kartikeya flies off on his peacock over mountains, seas and forests. Ganesha looks at his small mouse, then at his parents, and slowly walks around them three times, saying “you are my world.” Ganesha gets the fruit. Kartikeya returns, is hurt, and leaves for the south; he is worshipped as Murugan on the hill of Palani.

**Other version (for the caption):** in the Shiva Purana (Rudra Samhita, Kumara Khanda) the race decides which brother marries first. In Tamil tradition, Shiva and Parvati follow Kartikeya to the hill and say “pazham nee” (“you are the fruit”), which is how Palani is said to get its name.

**Lesson:** sometimes winning isn’t about going far, it’s about looking closer. Spend a little more time today with the people who are your world. Kartikeya is not mocked; he is honoured as Murugan.

## 7. Audio

- Narration: male Hindi voice, calm and warm, unhurried (the original used an AI voice: Kokoro, voice “hm_omega”, speed 0.9).
- Music: soft bansuri flute under the voice at about 20% volume, fading in over 2 s and out over the last 3 s. The original used “Indian Folk Classical Flute Music (1)” by MayankSingh33, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons.
- No sound effects needed; if you add any, keep them soft (a single bell when the fruit appears, wing flutter during the flight).

## 8. Rules for getting the gods right (important for AI video)

- **Ganesha:** elephant head, one tusk often broken, usually four arms, round belly, seated or walking calmly; his vehicle is a small mouse. Child-like and gentle in this story.
- **Kartikeya / Murugan:** a young warrior on a peacock, holding a spear (vel). In the race he is a youth and **unmarried**; do not show his consorts Valli and Devasena during the race (they belong to a later part of his story; the Palani shot may show the traditional six-faced Murugan image).
- **Shiva:** matted hair with the crescent moon, the river Ganga in his hair, third eye, a snake at the neck, trident nearby, ash-smeared pale or blue-grey skin, seated calm. **Parvati:** beside him, in red with a crown or veil.
- Setting: Kailash, mountains and forest. Narada (optional) is a sage with a veena.
- Check every frame for wrong numbers of arms or heads, extra fingers, melted faces, wrong vehicles (peacock for Kartikeya, mouse for Ganesha) and modern objects. Reject and regenerate rather than post a distorted god.
- No mockery of either brother, no violence, nothing that could read as disrespect.
- If the video has AI-generated audio or realistic AI visuals, turn on Instagram’s “AI info” label.

Most tools make 5 to 10 second clips, so generate one clip per shot, then edit them together with the narration. Put the style lock at the start of every prompt.

**Shot prompts:**

1. *Hook (6 s).* Split-screen portrait: on the left, the gentle face of child Ganesha with elephant head and golden crown; on the right, young Kartikeya with a red turban and a peacock behind him; a thin gold line divides the frame; a glowing golden mango floats on the line, bobbing softly. Halves drift slightly toward the centre.
2. *The fruit (8 s).* Shiva and Parvati seated together in a mountain forest on Kailash, Shiva with matted hair, crescent moon and a veena, Parvati in red beside him. A golden mango descends slowly from above and hovers between them, glowing. Slow push in.
3. *The challenge (8 s).* Close-up pan from Shiva’s calm face to Parvati’s face, painted style, soft light.
4. *The flight (9 s).* Young Kartikeya standing on his blue peacock, flying fast from right to left over painted mountains, then a painted ocean with curling waves, then a painted forest; wind streaks; his scarf flutters; the peacock’s tail streams behind.
5. *The mouse (7 s).* Ganesha seated on a low throne, camera glides down from his face to a small grey mouse sitting below the throne, who looks up at him; then Ganesha turns to look at his parents.
6. *Three circles (6 s).* Shiva and Parvati seated together, wide. Little Ganesha walks slowly around them three times, hands folded; his path leaves a glowing golden ring around the couple. Calm, devotional.
7. *“You are my world” (5 s).* Extreme close-up of Ganesha’s face and jewelled crown, eyes soft, very slow push in.
8. *The prize (4 s).* Pull back to reveal Ganesha seated; the glowing golden mango rests in his hands; specks of light rise.
9. *Palani (10 s).* Six-faced Murugan standing on his peacock with a cobra at its feet, on a green hill in South India; camera rises from the peacock’s feet to the faces.
10. *Looking closer (7 s).* Parvati holding baby Ganesha in her lap; slow push in to the baby’s face.
11. *Ending (9 s).* Shiva and Parvati seated together inside a faint glowing golden ring, moonlit; slow pull back; leave space at the bottom for text.
